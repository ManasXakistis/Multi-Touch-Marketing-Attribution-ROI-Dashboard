# Week 3 – Business Logic and KPI Definitions

# Objective

The objective of Week 3 is to translate raw, transactional advertising and campaign data into structured business logic that supports performance measurement and decision-making. This phase focuses on defining the core Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) used to evaluate marketing efficiency, designing a star schema to organize data for analytical querying, and establishing a repeatable data flow from raw ingestion to BI-ready reporting tables. The outcome of this week is a well-documented analytical foundation that downstream dashboards and stakeholder reports can rely on with confidence.

## KPI Definitions

### 1. Total Spend

- **Definition:** The total amount of money spent on advertising campaigns across all platforms and time periods.
- **Formula:** `Total Spend = SUM(Ad_Spend)`
- **Business Significance:** Total Spend is the baseline metric against which all efficiency ratios are calculated. It allows stakeholders to track budget utilization, compare spend across campaigns or platforms, and ensure marketing expenditure stays within approved limits.

### 2. Cost Per Click (CPC)

- **Definition:** The average cost incurred for each click generated by an advertising campaign.
- **Formula:** `CPC = Total Spend / Total Clicks`
- **Business Significance:** CPC indicates how efficiently a campaign converts spend into user engagement. A lower CPC generally reflects better-targeted or better-optimized campaigns, helping marketing teams identify which platforms or creatives deliver the most cost-effective traffic.

### 3. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

- **Definition:** The average cost required to acquire one paying or converting customer through advertising efforts.
- **Formula:** `CAC = Total Spend / Total Conversions`
- **Business Significance:** CAC is a critical profitability metric. It helps leadership understand whether the cost of acquiring customers is sustainable relative to customer lifetime value, and supports budget allocation decisions across campaigns and channels.

### 4. Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)

- **Definition:** The amount of revenue generated for every unit of currency spent on advertising.
- **Formula:** `ROAS = Revenue / Total Spend`
- **Business Significance:** ROAS is one of the most direct indicators of marketing profitability. It enables stakeholders to quickly assess whether a campaign is generating positive returns and to prioritize budget toward the highest-performing channels.

## Star Schema Design

The data model follows a star schema architecture, with a central fact table surrounded by descriptive dimension tables. This design optimizes the data for analytical queries and BI tool performance.

### Fact Table: `fact_campaign_performance`

- `performance_id` (Primary Key)
- `date_id` (Foreign Key → dim_date)
- `campaign_id` (Foreign Key → dim_campaign)
- `platform_id` (Foreign Key → dim_platform)
- `ad_spend`
- `clicks`
- `impressions`
- `conversions`
- `revenue`

### Dimension Table: `dim_date`

- `date_id` (Primary Key)
- `full_date`
- `day`
- `month`
- `quarter`
- `year`
- `day_of_week`
- `is_weekend`

### Dimension Table: `dim_campaign`

- `campaign_id` (Primary Key)
- `campaign_name`
- `campaign_type`
- `start_date`
- `end_date`
- `target_audience`
- `budget_allocated`

### Dimension Table: `dim_platform`

- `platform_id` (Primary Key)
- `platform_name`
- `platform_type`
- `region`

## Data Flow

The transformation of raw data into BI-ready KPIs follows a structured, multi-stage pipeline:

1. **Raw Data Ingestion:** Campaign-level data (ad spend, clicks, impressions, conversions, revenue) is collected from advertising platforms and loaded into a staging area.
2. **Data Cleaning and Validation:** Records are checked for duplicates, missing values, and inconsistent formatting using SQL and Python scripts. Invalid or incomplete records are flagged or corrected.
3. **Dimensional Modeling:** Cleaned data is mapped into the star schema, populating `dim_date`, `dim_campaign`, and `dim_platform`, and loading transactional metrics into `fact_campaign_performance`.
4. **KPI Calculation:** Business logic defined in this document (Total Spend, CPC, CAC, ROAS) is applied at the query layer or within scheduled SQL/Python jobs to produce aggregated, decision-ready metrics.
5. **BI Layer Integration:** The resulting KPI tables and views are connected to Power BI or Tableau, where they are visualized through dashboards and reports for stakeholders.
6. **Refresh and Maintenance:** Data refresh schedules ensure that KPIs remain current, with version control and documentation maintained alongside the underlying logic.

## Tools Used

- **SQL:** Used for data extraction, transformation, joining dimension and fact tables, and calculating KPI aggregations.
- **Python:** Used for data cleaning, validation, automation of ETL processes, and more advanced statistical or exploratory analysis.
- **Power BI / Tableau:** Used as the business intelligence layer to visualize KPIs, build interactive dashboards, and present insights to stakeholders.

## Conclusion

The business logic and KPI definitions established in Week 3 form the analytical backbone of the project. By clearly defining how Total Spend, CPC, CAC, and ROAS are calculated, and by organizing data into a clean star schema, this work ensures that reporting is consistent, auditable, and easy to maintain as the project scales. This foundation enables project stakeholders and decision-makers to trust the numbers presented in dashboards, compare performance across campaigns and platforms with confidence, and make informed budget and strategy decisions based on a single, well-documented source of truth.
